01 / THE WORLD

The old order
is unravelling.

The year is 1933. Recovery is uncertain. Political movements compete for the future. Every national decision reaches beyond its borders.

Pax Exilis explores this turning point through historical grand strategy. Its ambition is a world in which economics, politics, diplomacy, and military power are connected—where a budget can matter as much as a battle.

Originally developed as Atlas 1933, it is an evolving independent project. The setting is historical; the questions it invites are open-ended.

IN ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT
CHAPTER I

Atlas 1933

The project began under the name Atlas 1933: an exploration of a politically divided world and the systems that hold a country together.

CHAPTER II

Economics becomes a design problem.

Macroeconomic ideas, including Keynesian and classical approaches, inform the project’s economic direction. Turning theory into understandable player decisions remains a central challenge.

CHAPTER III

Complexity has a cost.

Map detail, simulation depth, and interface clarity compete for time and performance. Experiments, revisions, and reversions are part of the work. Earlier low-frame-rate problems are not presented as resolved.

CHAPTER IV

A new name. The same curiosity.

Pax Exilis carries the project forward from Atlas 1933. Built in Godot with C#, it remains a work in progress rather than a finished commercial release.

A curiosity about history.
A desire to understand its systems.

Leo Zhang creates and directs Pax Exilis, bringing together historical research, economic ideas, game design, and programming tools. AI-assisted development is part of the process, alongside the choices, testing, and revisions that shape the project.

The goal is both a strategy game and a continuing investigation: how can a simulation make the difficult decisions of a nation understandable?
